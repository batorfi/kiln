◆Decider

decides every gate with a ten-part brief — approve, revise, reject, restart, split; arms delegation deliberately
e.g. restarts r10 at Code review: “check the hold flag inside the lane”

≡Roadmap shaper

writes goals and constraints first, shapes the draft with the planner, pins rows, admits one feature
e.g. pins “Event backbone” before real workers

✎Thinker at the bench

researches, reads code and prototypes while factories work; notes become knowledge with their origin
e.g. spikes the swap cost before admitting “Pin judges”

⇄Teammate

votes on the team roadmap, claims paths, answers opinion requests, reviews teammates’ pull requests
e.g. resolves an overlap with Anna on shared/config.ts

❖Curator

keeps the knowledge base true: answers the keeper’s questions, resolves contradictions, reviews drift
e.g. answers “why does billing/legacy/ exist?”

⚙Operator

configures models, roles, flows, hosts and policies as files; reads health; places factories
e.g. adds a security reviewer, tries it on a past feature
🧑‍💻

You

the software engineer — owner of the workshop, reviewer of record

What only you do: admit features · decide gates · say where models run · delegate on purpose · keep knowledge true

Only you

  • admit what gets built — at the Roadmap gate, one feature at a time
  • decide every gate, with the whole picture in the brief
  • choose where models run and what may leave the machine
  • delegate routine gates on purpose — any objection gives them back
  • keep the knowledge true, so the next decision is better informed

Never your chore

  • driving each step — the director keeps factories moving by rule
  • watching screens — what needs you comes to one place, and waits
  • remembering context — every brief is self-contained
  • writing the record — every decision is recorded with who and why
  • chasing teammates — claims show overlap before it becomes a conflict
